# Resolve conflicting records with task-dependent depth

Answer the actual question using the supplied rules and evidence. Use the following routing and safeguards without imposing the same procedure on every task. This workflow is self-contained and requires no tools or other skills.

## 1. Establish scope and choose a path

Identify the entity, attribute, requested time or version, source authority, and required answer format. Inspect the full supplied material for relevant conflicts and revisions, including later items that target earlier evidence. Distinguish task instructions from statements within the records.

**Direct path:** If no relevant competing claims or revisions require resolution, use the ordinary task method. Skip the dependency ledger, status classification, competing-claim challenge, and restoration analysis below. A long record alone does not require those steps. Separate authorized actions remain separate even when their wording or effects match.

**Conflict path:** If a conflict or revision can affect the answer, apply section 2 to the affected evidence and its dependencies. Irrelevant disputes do not require reprocessing otherwise unambiguous evidence. Return to the ordinary task method once validity is resolved.

For either path, use sections 3 and 4. They do not require a printed table or a fixed calculation strategy.

## 2. Resolve consequential conflicts

Keep identifiers attached to claims and operations. When useful, keep a compact record of source, scope, operation, revision target, and effective status for disputed items and their dependencies. Include revisions of revisions even if they do not directly answer the question.

Distinguish different scopes or effective periods, reports of events, independent events, competing assertions, and explicit revisions. A repeated report does not establish a second event; matching text does not establish that independently identified events are duplicates. Apply only the authority and priority rules supplied. Confidence, repetition, and recency alone do not establish precedence.

For each revision, verify its authority, exact target, scope, timing, and whether it remains effective. Resolve the complete relevant dependency chain. Cancellation can erase an earlier action, end its effect prospectively, or require compensation; use the stated semantics. Canceling a cancellation restores the earlier item only when those rules imply it. Do not both remove an action and reverse it again. Leave unrelated evidence unchanged.

Retain material uncertainty when a missing reference or unresolved rule actually changes the answer. Do not invent a conflict or a tie-break rule.

## 3. Derive and verify the answer

Apply each effective contribution once, preserving scope, units, direction, and order when required. Distinguish setting a state from changing it and reporting a state from changing it.

Keep the task's natural derivation method. Segmenting, regrouping, and reverse-checking are optional aids, not mandatory extra passes. If intermediate results help, keep their boundaries and contributing source items clear. Include the starting state once and reach the requested endpoint. Historical states and accumulations of finally effective items are different; compare like with like.

Check evidence selection, local operations, and final aggregation separately. A sum of intermediate results can be arithmetically consistent while an intermediate result is unsupported by its records. A repeated final answer does not verify either. When a discrepancy appears, revisit the relevant source items and correct the specific selection or operation before updating dependent results. If the first discrepancy is earlier than the final segment, checking only that last segment cannot resolve it. Do not revise a supported result merely to satisfy an expectation or produce an elaborate audit.

## 4. Deliver

Follow the requested answer format. When an explanation is allowed, state the result and decisive evidence concisely. A full ledger or segment table is unnecessary unless requested or useful for communicating a material discrepancy. Do not claim an independent check that was not performed. Distinguish unresolved evidence from unfinished calculation; state a material uncertainty precisely when it cannot be resolved from the supplied rules.
